VISUAL AND 3D INTERFACE DIRECTION

Aim for a believable contemporary office/security facility with realistic scale,
materials, lighting and purposeful equipment. The current block-like furniture,
coarse plants, oversized bars and generic architectural details still limit fidelity.
The presence of textured people and product logos does not mean the scene is finished.

Treat the building as the operating interface:
- Choose a workstation, gateway, rack, storage system or console directly in 3D.
- Show a clear placement preview on an eligible surface/slot, explain the target and
  cost, and visibly install the selected capability after a successful purchase.
- Make equipment silhouettes and installation locations distinguishable at a useful
  camera distance. Use ports, bays, screens, cabling and scale where they convey role.
- Show important state changes on the actual affected host, rack, room or console:
  installed agent, observed activity, isolation, service disruption, recovery progress
  and restored service. Drive every indication from real engine state.
- Let an XDR console link evidence to the real originating devices. Represent storage
  recovery at the relevant storage/affected service. Preserve the sensor limitations.
- Use restrained, temporary feedback and selection emphasis. Keep labels contextual
  to selection/hover/zoom rather than floating banners over every object.
- Provide smooth, purposeful focus/orbit transitions that keep targets visible and
  preserve orientation. Validate occlusion on both floors and at close range.
- Keep an overview and a quick return from detailed interaction. Camera movement must
  never prevent an urgent response, continue after manual orbit, or trap mobile users.

Move suitable interaction into the scene rather than simply drawing more beveled
cards on top of it. Equipment consoles, contextual object actions and spatial evidence
are useful 3D UI. Decorative rotating logos and permanent neon effects add little.

Keep essential time, budget, pause, language and navigation controls readable. Use a
small contextual HTML overlay for legible text and accessible controls when appropriate;
do not turn every paragraph into a texture or make critical controls depend on tiny
world-space text. Keyboard/touch equivalents must remain available for scene actions.
Reuse the existing inspector/deck or replace their functions coherently; avoid parallel
interfaces that expose the same information twice. Do not grow the permanent HUD.

Product representations must remain accurate in meaning:
- AF/IAG: relevant gateway equipment, with their actual scope and limitations.
- STA/NDR: a network probe; detection is distinct from endpoint blocking.
- EDR: software on the chosen host, including independent installations on both floors.
- XDR: an evidence/correlation console, not automatic omniscient detection.
- MDR: a managed service accessed through a console, not a fictional MDR appliance.
- aStor: snapshot storage, with preparation and restoration represented truthfully.
- HCI/ADC: infrastructure with visible redundancy/load-distribution roles.
- SASE: the controlled remote-access policy/endpoint scope actually used by the engine.
- AICP: the local automation console and supported helper rules; no fabricated online AI.

Existing deployment models are illustrative representatives, not replicas of specific
commercial appliances. Improve their art without inventing official model specifications.
Preserve official mark artwork, colors within the source artwork and aspect ratios.
Use the official Solvia name/panda. Do not invent replacement product logos or mascots.

Build one coherent, polished room/interaction sample first, inspect it in the real
browser, then apply that standard consistently across the playable building. Align
chairs, seated poses, desks, monitors and feet. Improve furniture proportions, bevels,
glass/metal/fabric/wood finishes, grounding and lighting as one visual system. Keep the
existing textured business characters. Use authorized local assets or authored models;
record provenance and optimize them before inclusion. The game must not require an
asset-service login, temporary signed download URL or a paid runtime API.

GAMEPLAY DEVELOPMENT REQUIREMENTS

Audit the complete loop, then implement at least two coherent, meaningful gameplay
improvements connected to the existing missions. Choose a focused combination of
replayable scenario variation and richer response decisions; do not attempt every
possible subsystem. A mechanic must change player choices, consequences or replay
value rather than only add an animation, a button or another counter.

Useful directions include:
- Briefing-supported, reproducible variations in foothold, route or attack sequence,
  so memorizing one scripted response is less effective. Keep event ordering causal.
- Evidence that develops through observation and corroboration, with uncertainty that
  can be resolved through available actions. Keep visible clues distinguishable and
  explanations tied to sensors the player actually deployed.
- More meaningful tradeoffs between protecting a particular endpoint, maintaining
  service, preserving data and containing the actor. Make downtime/recovery costs and
  limited response resources legible before commitment where the player could know.
- Branching consequences from targeted isolation, advance restrictions, failed remote
  access, prepared recovery and delayed response. Let the scene show the consequences.
- Clearer objectives and debrief explanations that connect the player's own decisions
  to actual incidents, missed opportunities and honest partial success.

Choose what fits the existing architecture and demonstrate the effect in real play.
Avoid grind, arbitrary timers, redundant currencies, upgrade trees or new modes that
do not improve this security-response loop. Do not turn every product into a tower
that attacks enemies, and do not imply capabilities unsupported by the product model.

Maintain viable budget-correct strategies for all nine assignment/difficulty pairs.
An undefended run should still lose for understandable reasons. Prevent duplicate
spending, invalid targets, hidden prerequisite failures and purchases that silently
do nothing. Preserve mission-derived operation relevance without revealing hidden
attack truth. Difficulty should change decisions and pressure while remaining fair.

Every new mechanic needs clear EN/ZH explanation, scene feedback, failure feedback,
undo/reset behavior where applicable, and accurate replay/report/debrief handling.
Keep the local helper subordinate to the same evidence, preparation and resource rules.

PERFORMANCE AND IMPLEMENTATION DISCIPLINE

Responsiveness is a release requirement. Do not trade away playability for appearance.
Preserve static batching, shared resources and geometry merged by material. Avoid
per-port/per-screw draw calls, duplicated textures, repeated full-screen effects,
unbounded particle systems and continuous decorative animation. Keep permanent lights
and shadow costs controlled. Freeze paused animation and skip hidden document work.

Keep stable DOM controls connected during unrelated state updates; preserve focus,
scroll position and touch interaction. Do not repaint the whole interface every frame.
Use one simulation clock; pause, speed, deadlines and recovery must stay consistent.
Keep new code modular and remove superseded implementations instead of keeping two.
Retain the current static delivery model unless a concrete requirement justifies change.

Measure on the same hardware, viewport, DPR, camera and scene before/after changes.
Use a normal affordable deployed plan, not only an empty scene. Record frame-time
distribution, draw calls, triangles and loading/memory behavior across repeated resets.
Target stable frame pacing and no material regression; optimize a regression before
expanding the art sample. Treat consistent 60 FPS as a target, not an unmeasured claim.

Reference sample on Intel Iris Xe / Direct3D11, 1440x900, DPR 1, fixed overview,
setup and stationary staff: the five-purchase scene measured about 52.7 FPS current
versus 55.8 FPS prior mainline, with 282 versus 450 draw calls and p95 near 25 ms.
These are short samples, not guarantees or evidence of improvement on every machine.
Check actual laptop DPR and a mobile viewport as well. Use software WebGL for
functional checks only; never present its FPS as representative hardware performance.
Run local GPU-heavy browser tests sequentially and close unused test browsers.
